Russian research on post-quantum signatures includes the development of hash-based schemes using domestic primitives, exemplified by Hypericum. The
compact isogeny-based signature SQIsign offers a different trade-off between transmitted data size, computational cost, and cryptographic assumptions. This
work addresses the design and evaluation of a consistent SQIsign 3.0 profile using Streebog and Russian pseudorandom sequence generation mechanisms. The
objective is to assess adaptation costs and the conditions under which compact keys and signatures retain practical value. The profile covers Fiat–Shamir
challenge hashing and internal pseudorandom streams. These components are considered separately because their purposes, secrecy requirements, streaming
semantics, and effects on compatibility differ. The analysis covers input encodings, output truncation, domain separation, entropy requirements, and the limits of
applicability of the original scheme’s security arguments. Experimental comparison of the reference implementation, individual substitutions, and their
combination covers key generation, signing, and verification times, and memory consumption. Comparison with selected Hypericum parameter sets accounts for
signing limits and differences in security estimates. The contribution comprises a consistent specification of the adaptation of public hashing and secret
pseudorandom streams, an analysis of its compatibility and security implications, and an assessment of substitution costs.
